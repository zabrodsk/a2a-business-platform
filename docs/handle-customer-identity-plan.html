Implemented and deployed 9 October 2026 · shared access verified with a real customer Grok Bot

One customer account. Access to every participating business.

The customer signs into Handle, authorizes their personal agent once, and asks it to find a service. Each participating business accepts that identity and creates its own customer record without another signup or password.

This is an execution aid. The canonical specification, version 2.3, §6.6 and T44–T51 owns the requirements. Shared customer access is implemented alongside the previous per-business claim. See authentication and deployment configuration and verification evidence. The customer portal is live. Synthetic HTTP/browser checks and a real customer-runtime check have separate evidence; two separate Grok Bot accounts and live-runtime purchasing remain unproven.

Customer experience

  1. Sign into Handle
    One human account and an explicit personal-agent connection.
  2. Ask for a service
    The agent discovers a participating business and requests limited access.
  3. Get an offer
    The business recognizes the customer without a local login.
  4. Approve the booking
    The existing human mandate and business rules control the commitment.

Within the existing consent, visiting the second participating business introduces no new signup, password or mandatory repeat agent-linking ceremony. New permissions, additional profile sharing or an expired human session can still require a Handle decision. Booking approval remains separate from agent access.

First-version scope

  • One persistent customer identity in the team-hosted Handle service, separate from owner membership and legacy accounts.
  • A human consent screen to link a personal agent, permit activities and choose which contact fields it may share.
  • A separate revocable grant for each participating business. The first version supports two isolated service origins trusting one configured Handle issuer.
  • Business-specific credentials lasting at most ten minutes, renewed within a valid delegation. Every protected operation checks current delegation and grant state.
  • Idempotent local customer creation, with a business-specific stable identifier and no new password. Existing account history requires a separate ownership proof.
  • Customer mandate approval through Handle, mapped to the correct local customer and case without a legacy login.
  • A minimal Handle access page listing the personal agent, business grants and shared fields, with per-business and whole-agent revocation.

Excluded: unrelated websites, new CRM adapters, a second business vertical, a new payment provider, KYC, automatic email-based account merging, universal trust in agent providers, shared payment wallets and production identity-marketplace features. Reuse existing modules and dependencies; a new dependency requires an explicit user request.

Trust and data contract

Handle stores the persistent human customer subject, agent principal/connection, consent version and expiry, permitted activities and shared fields. Each business grant stores its target business and resource audience, scopes, consent reference and revocation state. Each business stores a unique mapping from trusted issuer plus business-specific subject to its local customer ID. Tokens identify the acting agent as well as the represented customer; a profile email is never the identity key.

A participating backend explicitly configures the trusted Handle issuer and its validation mechanism. Public registry entries and Agent Card metadata discover services; they cannot add trusted issuers. Validate issuer, audience, business, actor connection, expiry and granted scopes before creating a local record or reading data. Check live delegation/grant state on every protected operation and fail closed if that check is unavailable.

Human session cookies stay on Handle. Business APIs receive only their resource-specific agent credentials. The client must not send the Handle session or a business credential to another origin or follow a credential-bearing redirect. A central customer identity survives agent replacement; each replacement connection needs its own human consent.

Use established authentication and delegation contracts, with OpenID Connect and OAuth token exchange as design references. Before implementation, verify current official documentation and choose the supported flow using the existing runtime and dependencies. Do not advertise OIDC conformance, token-exchange grants or metadata until implemented and tested.

Implementation sequence

  1. Lock down the boundaries and regression baseline. Inspect current concurrent changes and existing authentication tests. Confirm the Handle issuer origin, two business resource origins and the exact human login/delegation protocol. Define validation schemas in contracts; add meaningful negative tests before changing authentication. Keep local per-business claim behavior covered.
  2. Add the shared customer account and agent consent. Extend the Handle persistence and HTTP boundary referenced above, or add narrowly scoped customer modules beside them. Separate customer account creation from the owner-only setup gate. Store a consent request bound to the human, connection, permitted actions and sharing; reject CSRF, replay and expired requests. Provide a small customer login/consent/access UI rather than a new chat interface.
  3. Issue and validate business access; create local records. Add a central business-grant/credential service and an integration beside AgentAuth. Register each trusted business resource explicitly. Create the local mapping transactionally under a unique constraint; retries and restarts return the same record. Do not match old accounts by email. Wire the same verified actor into A2A and customer tools.
  4. Carry human decisions and revocation through the business boundary. Let a customer Handle session approve the exact business/case mandate after verifying ownership and version; record the server-verified human decision for the existing policy checks. Add per-business and whole-connection revocation, persistent generation checks, and denied renewal after revocation. Keep accepted bookings and payment recovery under their original authorizations.
  5. Prove the two-business journey and update runtime instructions. Run two isolated instances of the existing business backend with separate origins and storage, one shared Handle identity service and synthetic records. The same agent obtains two offers without further signup/login; Pneu completes one authorized local-demo booking. Update the customer prompt, discovery skill and auth documentation only when their advertised route actually works. Record automated and real GrokBot evidence separately.

The existing Handle implementation shares a process with legacy and currently registers only its own installation. The central customer issuer must be shared by both demo instances rather than creating two unrelated “Handle accounts.” This is the main integration task; the second instance is an isolation fixture, not a new product.

Risks and mitigations

  • Account takeover through email matching: use issuer/subject mappings and a separate ownership proof for existing records; never grant historical access by email.
  • Cross-business token reuse: enforce business and audience in both relay and tools, use separate grants and credentials, and test distinct origins/storage.
  • Owner/customer role confusion: separate customer signup/session permissions from owner setup and membership; deny agent tokens on human consent endpoints.
  • Revocation lag or issuer outage: check active delegation on each protected operation and deny new work if unavailable. Recover already accepted operations under their stored authorization.
  • Hidden repeated signup during mandate approval: include a real Handle human approval in the end-to-end flow; counting only token issuance is insufficient.
  • Unbounded identity infrastructure work: one issuer, two instances of the existing backend, existing dependencies and local-demo payment only. Broader provider/CRM federation is excluded.
